One syscall through the seccomp filter

Before your command starts, minibox gives the kernel a small classic-BPF program, and the kernel runs it on every syscall. These are the real instructions our compiler emits, for the policy generated from the attack suite's benign service and for Docker's default profile.

Every traced syscall, both filters

Seccomp is one layer of several. Docker's baseline lets ptrace() through, yet the ptrace attack below is blocked under baseline: without CAP_SYS_PTRACE, Yama (ptrace_scope=1) only lets a process trace its own descendants. Likewise the generated policy allows socket(), but the raw-socket attack fails under it because the policy keeps no CAP_NET_RAW. The table also shows the cost of a linear filter. Docker's profile runs hundreds of instructions for a syscall near the end of its list, while the short policy never runs more than a few dozen.
